## 0B. Gazette texts behind the compendia (added 24.10.2026)

FSSAI says a compendium is not a legal document. Each library folder that holds a compendium now also holds, in a subfolder named `Gazette texts behind the compendium`, the principal Gazette notification and every amendment the compendium has absorbed (73 files; list and status in the register). The rule:

1. **In an audit report**, cite the compendium page, as the reading text, and add the Gazette source beside it in the Sources table.
2. **In a notice, reply, opinion or pleading**, cite the Gazette text. A provision the compendium shows without an amendment marker is cited from the principal notification. A provision the compendium marks `N[ ]` comes from amendment No. N in the compendium's closing note (L&D: 1 = 10.09.2021, 2 = 09.09.2022, 3 = 11.10.2022, 4 = 08.08.2025; A&C: 1 = 09.10.2020, 2 = 30.08.2022, 3 = 13.12.2022; FPS&FA: the amendment number in `22 Amendment number reference`, mapped in the register). In the N16 compendium, text marked `1[ ]` or "Amendment for substitution / insertion" is from the First Amendment of 06.09.2021.

## 0A. Which law is in force on the audit date

Before any provision is applied, check that it is in force on the date of the audit.

1. Labelling changes in any FSS Regulations come into force on 1 July, after at least 365 days from notification; emergencies are decided case by case. Order of 06.01.2026 (48th Food Authority meeting), ADV 08.2, p. 1, para 2. It supersedes the order of 03.01.2025.
2. Notified but not yet in force:

| Instrument | In force from | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Contaminants Amendment Regulations, 2026 (No. 17): lead and cadmium in pulse flours, inorganic arsenic in fish oils | 01.12.2026 | CT, 14.05.2026_Amendment No. 17, reg. 1(2) (Hindi p. 2, English p. 4) |
| Vegan Foods Second Amendment Regulations, 2026: Form I certificate for imported vegan products | 01.04.2027 | VEG, 28.09.2026_Amendment, reg. 1(2), p. 4 |
| Labelling and Display First Amendment Regulations, 2026 | 01.07.2027 | LD-26, reg. 1(2), p. 5 |
| Vegan Foods Amendment Regulations, 2026: new vegan logo | 01.07.2027 | VEG, 21.05.2026_Amendment, reg. 1(2), p. 3 |
| Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026, G.S.R. 789(E) | 01.01.2027 | library 11, 09.09.2026_Amendment, r. 1(2), p. 5 (read from the page image) |
| LM (PC) Second Amendment Rules, 2026, G.S.R. 312(E): r. 6(10A) substituted | 01.07.2027 | LM-G, 27.04.2026, r. 1(2), p. 2 |

From each of these dates the instrument applies automatically; no further instruction is needed. Before that date it is not applied, and the report may note it under Recommended course as a coming change.

3. The **Nutra Regulations exist in two texts**, and a label is checked against both:
   - **N22**, the draft FSS (Health Supplements, Nutraceuticals, FSDU, FSMP and Prebiotic and Probiotic Food) Regulations, 2022, operationalised by Direction dated 29.03.2022 (N22 p. 3, para 3: it "shall supersede" the 2016 Regulations) and last re-operationalised by Direction dated 30.08.2024. The FSSAI archive lists no later Direction (checked 23.10.2026).
   - **N16**, the FSS (Health Supplements, Nutraceuticals, FSDU, FSMP, Functional Food and Novel Food) Regulations, 2016 as amended in 2021 (amendment in force 06.09.2021, compliance by 01.04.2022). FSSAI enforces N16 as current law in its Advisory of 15.10.2026 (Schedule IV and regulations 3(13), 4(2), 6(2)(i), 7(2)(i), 7(3)(v), 8(2)(i), 9(2)(i), 12) and its Advisory of 16.04.2026 (Ashwagandha), and in Annexure I of the ePAAS Order of 06.05.2026; regulation 5(3)(c)(xiii) of the Labelling and Display Regulations (LD p. 12) also refers to N16.
   - The 2021 amendment substituted N16 Schedules IV, VI and VII with lists that carry the same entry numbers and ranges as N22 Schedules II, III and IV. So the composition test gives the same answer under both texts; the labelling provisions differ (section 2A). Where they differ, the report states both and says which is stricter.

## 2. Nutra Regulations, N22 (draft 2022 text operated by Directions)

PINNED from N22 (Annexure 1 at pp. 4 to 19, Schedules at pp. 20 to 70, Annexure 3 at pp. 71 to 79). The file carries, in order: p. 1 the Direction of 18.10.2022; p. 2 the Direction of 10.05.2022 (seven additives from N16 Schedule VE allowed at GMP; Schedule III enzymes and proteins allowed in a health supplement); p. 3 the Direction of 29.03.2022. The later Directions are in N22-D: 11.04.2023 (from 01.04.2023), 17.11.2023 (from 01.10.2023), 30.08.2024 (from 01.07.2024, enclosing 05.06.2024, which also allows Codex additives and the GMP Table in all categories and formats). Cite as: the draft FSS (Nutra) Regulations, 2022, operationalised by Direction dated 29.03.2022 and last re-operationalised by Direction dated 30.08.2024.

### Regulation 2, scope (N22 p. 4)

- **2(1)** Foods for population above 2 years, consumed orally in defined quantities and duration; not parenteral.
- **2(3)** Standardised foods and plants or botanicals offered in normal or naturally occurring form are not HS, Nutra, FSDU or FSMP.
- **2(4)** Not a drug as defined in s. 3(a), (b) and (h) of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940.
- **2(5)** No hormones, steroids, narcotic drug or psychotropic substance (NDPS Act Schedule), or substances in Schedules E and E-1 of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945.
- **2(6)** Products claiming cure, prevention or mitigation of any specific disease, disorder or condition fall outside these regulations unless specifically permitted.
- **2(7)** Mere food forms, minimally processed (cleaned, de-weeded, sorted, dried or powdered), are not HS or Nutra.

### Regulation 3, definitions (N22 p. 5)

- **3(e)** Health supplement: a concentrated source of nutrients and/or other ingredients with nutritional or physiological effects, whose purpose is to supplement the normal diet.
- **3(g)** Nutraceutical: extracts, isolates and purified chemical compounds having a physiological benefit and helping to maintain health.

### Regulation 5, general requirements (N22 pp. 6 to 10)

- **5(1)(a)** Delivery formats include tablets, capsules, powders, gummies and others (p. 6). **5(1)(b)** a conventional format (bar, biscuit, candy) must not be represented as conventional food.
- **5(2)(a)** Schedule I nutrients, II plants or botanicals, III molecules, isolates and extracts other than Schedule II, IV prebiotics and probiotics. Footnote: a Schedule II ingredient may be used as such or as extract; an extract is subject to the extractive ratio in relation to the daily usage value and **shall be obtained only from the part of the plant listed in the Schedule** (p. 6).
- **5(2)(c)** IFCT cereals, legumes, fruits, vegetables and Spices Board spices may be used as such or as extracts (p. 6).
- **5(2)(d)** Non-specified foods and ingredients need approval under the NSF&FI Regulations, 2017 (p. 6).
- **5(3)(a)** Ingredients with documented incident-free history of safe use (30 years in the country of origin or 15 years in India) only with prior approval (pp. 6 to 7).
- **5(4)(a), (b)** Additives as in Appendix A and C of FSS (FPS&FA); for tablet, capsule, syrup and pill formats see Annexure 3. **5(4)(d)** any other additive needs prior approval. **5(4)(e)** esters and salts of vitamins, in particular C and E, at GMP subject to the maximum; where none is specified, the total shall not exceed the ICMR RDA (p. 7).
- **5(5)(b)** Where purity standards are not specified: IP, API, USP, BP, BIS, ICMR Quality Standards of Indian Medicinal Plants, JECFA or Codex (p. 7).
- **5(6)** Any single purified chemical entity listed, except extracts of plants or botanicals and vitamins, minerals, amino acids and nucleotides, is not to be sold as HS, Nutra, FSDU, FSMP or Pre-Pro without prior approval (p. 8).
- **5(7)** "Any combination of ingredients in a formulation shall be based on available scientific and technical evidence; and such evidences shall be made available to the Food Authority as and when called for." The whole of the composition rationality case. Cite it every time (p. 8).
- **5(8)(b)** Tablet or capsule quality, where not specified, per the IP general monograph, if applicable (p. 8).
- **5(9)(a)** Only food grade solvent, singly or in combination, for extraction of plants or botanicals; **5(9)(b)** any other solvent needs prior approval under NSF&FI (p. 8).
- **5(10)** Products for children of 2 to 5 years only under medical advice by a recognised medical doctor, dietician or nutritionist (p. 8).
- **5(11)(a)** Overages permitted on scientific rationale, as specified by the Food Authority; **5(11)(b)** analytical tolerance as per the L&D Regulations, 2020 (p. 8). The overage cap (Table C) and the duty to declare **"appropriate overages added" in the composition declaration panel** are in Schedule I, Note 2 (p. 23), not in 5(11).
- **5(14)(a)** Contaminants (CT), microbial (Appendix B, FPS&FA), packaging (PK). **5(14)(b)** preparation and handling per Schedule 4 of the L&R Regulations, 2011 (LR pp. 36 to 54, Part II from p. 42; para 5.2.5 substituted by the Amendment of 23.06.2026). For a food the GMP reference point is this Schedule 4, not Schedule M of the Drugs Rules (p. 10).
- **5(15)(a)** A food suitable for a dietary regimen by its natural composition shall not be designated "health supplement"; it may say "this food is by its nature X" (p. 10).

### Regulation 5(12), labelling (N22 pp. 8 to 9)

Opening words: "In addition to the general labelling requirements specified under FSS (Labelling and Display) Regulations, 2020".

**5(12)(a)(i), front of pack (p. 8)**
- (A) "HEALTH SUPPLEMENT / NUTRACEUTICAL / FOOD FOR SPECIAL DIETARY USE / FOOD FOR SPECIAL MEDICAL PURPOSE / PREBIOTIC FOOD / PROBIOTIC FOOD" as applicable, **in capital and bold letters in the immediate proximity of the name or brand name**.
- (B) A prominent statement of the target consumer group and/or age group, if formulated for a specific age group.

**5(12)(a)(ii), front or back of pack (p. 9)**
- (A) "NOT FOR MEDICINAL USE" in capital and bold letters prominently written, unless exempted for specific categories (FSDU and FSMP are exempted, 6(3)(f) and 6(4)(f)).
- (B) Recommended usage level.
- (C) Duration of usage, where applicable.
- (D) "Not to exceed the recommended daily usage", prominently written.
- (E) Advisory warning where a danger may exist with excess consumption.
- (F) Warning on precautions, known side effects, contraindications and published product or drug interactions.
- (G) "product is not to be used as a substitute for a varied diet", except FSDU and FSMP.
- (H) "product is required to be stored out of reach of children".
- (I) Quantity of nutrients as a percentage of the relevant RDA, unless exempted by another regulation.

**5(12)(a)(iii), front, back or accompanying leaflet (p. 9)**
- (A) Amount of the nutrients or substances with a nutritional or physiological effect.
- (B) Sufficient information on the nature and purpose of the food, with detailed instructions and precautions for use.

**5(12)(b)** Any further category-specific labelling also applies.

### Regulation 5(13), claims (N22 p. 9)

- **(a)** No claim to treat, cure, mitigate or prevent any specific disease, disorder or condition, or reference to such properties, unless specifically permitted.
- **(b)** Structure, function or general well-being statements may be allowed if supported by generally accepted scientific data.
- **(c)** Nutritional, health or disease risk reduction claims only as listed under the Advertising and Claims Regulations, 2018; any other claim, or exemption from AC reg. 10(1), needs prior approval. Since 01.06.2026 every such application is filed only on ePAAS (Order of 06.05.2026, ADV 08.2, paras 3 and 5).

### Regulation 6, category specific (N22 pp. 10 to 19)

- **6(1)(a)** Health supplements supplement the normal diet and are not intended to treat or cure any deficiency (p. 10).
- **6(1)(b)** Ingredients from the schedules "(except Schedule III)" (p. 10); the Direction of 10.05.2022 (N22 p. 2) allows Schedule III enzymes and proteins in a health supplement.
- **6(1)(c)(i)** Nutrients: not more than the level specified; otherwise minimum 15 per cent of the ICMR RDA where a nutrient content claim is made, not less than 30 per cent for a higher content claim, and **not exceeding one RDA in any case** (pp. 10 to 11). **6(1)(c)(ii)** ingredient limits as in the Schedule; where none is set, the FBO adopts a level on scientific data and keeps the evidence (p. 11).
- **6(1)(f)(i)** labelling per 5(12); **6(1)(f)(ii)** "Health supplement" may be interchangeably termed "Dietary supplement" or "Food supplement" (p. 11).
- **6(2)(a)** Nutraceuticals provide a physiological benefit and are not intended to treat or cure any medical condition, disease or disorder (p. 11).
- **6(2)(b)** Schedule III molecules, isolates and extracts, with ingredients from other Schedules as optional ingredients (p. 11).
- **6(2)(c)(i)** "Limits as specified in Schedule III with standardisation to marker compounds specified and at daily usage levels specified therein"; where no level is set, the FBO adopts one on scientific data and keeps the evidence; where no marker is specified, the ingredient meets the manufacturer specification and purity criteria (p. 11). **6(2)(c)(ii)** nutrients as in 6(1)(c)(i), not exceeding one RDA (pp. 11 to 12).
- **6(2)(f)** Labelling: "Shall comply with general requirements 5(12)" (p. 12).
- **6(3)** FSDU (pp. 12 to 15), **6(4)** FSMP (pp. 15 to 18) and **6(5)** Pre-Pro (pp. 18 to 19) carry their own labelling lists; probiotic food must declare genus, species and strain, viable count at end of shelf life, serving size, storage (6(5)(f), p. 19).

**K.S. Saini and Anr. v. Union of India**, AIR 1967 P&H 322, Civil Writ No. 315-D of 1966, decided 19.10.1966. PINNED from DMR. Holdings used by Block R, point 165: (i) the advertisement must refer to a drug and its terms must suggest or be calculated to lead to its use for the prohibited purpose, the suggestion being to third persons (para 4); (ii) a statement that the advertiser treats listed diseases by scientific methods, naming no drug, is not a suggestion to use any drug (para 4); (iii) the pamphlet is read as a whole, but medicine names in one part are not read as suggesting a cure for ailments listed in another (para 4); (iv) a general tonic for weakness is not a suggestion of use for sexual pleasure (para 6); (v) a penal law is construed strictly (para 6); (vi) where the complaint at face value makes out no offence, the proceeding is quashed, R.P. Kapur v. State of Punjab, AIR 1960 SC 866 followed (para 3).
