# Schedule lookup

Sources: N22 Annexure 2 (library file `02 Nutra Regulations 2022\18.10.2022_Direction_Nutra Regulations 2022 text (with 29.03.2022, 10.05.2022, Annexures).pdf`) and N16 as amended in 2021 (`02 Nutra Regulations 2022\02.1 2016 Regulations (as amended 2021)\29.09.2021_Compendium_2016 Regulations as amended (Version I).pdf`). Page numbers are PDF pages of those files. Section numbers of provisions refer to `01_provisions.md`.

**Coverage.** This file carries the structural rules, Schedule I, Schedule III Part A in full, the caution statements and the entries most often met. The complete Schedule II (439 botanicals, every part and range), the complete Schedule III Part B (192 entries) and Schedule IV (16 prebiotics, 31 probiotic organisms) are in `02A_schedules_full.md`, each entry with its page in both texts; an ingredient is looked up there before any judgment is formed. An ingredient in none of the tables is non-specified (section 1) and the finding is UTV pending the client's NSF&FI approval; no entry is ever assumed.

**Both texts give the same entry.** The 2021 amendment substituted N16 Schedules IV, VI and VII with lists that carry the same entry numbers and ranges as N22 Schedules II, III and IV. Cite both, for example "N22 Sch. II entry 432 (p. 57); N16 Sch. IV entry 432 (p. 112)". The original 2016 lists printed earlier in the N16 compendium (Schedule IV at pp. 38 to 59, Schedule VI at pp. 130 to 137) are superseded; their numbers and ranges differ and are never cited.

### The category consequence

- **Health Supplement**, N22 6(1)(b) (N16 6(2)(i)): Schedules I, II and IV, **not Schedule III**, except that Schedule III enzymes and proteins are allowed by the Direction of 10.05.2022 (N22 p. 2).
- **Nutraceutical**, N22 6(2)(b) (N16 7(2)(i)): Schedule III is its home; ingredients of the other Schedules may be added as optional ingredients.

One Schedule III ingredient (other than an enzyme or protein) on a pack declared "HEALTH SUPPLEMENT" is a category defect, and it usually carries the whole audit.

### Where a Part B entry has no number

N22 6(2)(c)(i) second limb (N16 7(3)(iii) and (iv)): the FBO adopts the usage level on relevant scientific data and keeps the evidence, to be produced when called for. A Part B ingredient is never "unlimited"; the burden moves from the Schedule to the client's file, and N22 5(7) (N16 3(29)) then does the work.

## 2. Schedule I, nutrients (N22 pp. 20 to 24)

Vitamins A, provitamin A, B1, B2, B6, B12, C, D, E, K1, K2 (MK-7 and MK-4), biotin, folic acid, niacin, pantothenic acid, each with permitted chemical sources. Minerals: calcium, chloride, chromium, copper, iodine, iron, magnesium, manganese, molybdenum, phosphorus, potassium, selenium, sodium, zinc, boron. Amino acids, other nutrients and nucleotides follow.

**Vitamin C** permitted sources include L-ascorbic acid, sodium, calcium and potassium L-ascorbate, ascorbyl palmitate. A label saying only "Vitamin C" without the source is incomplete for a composition panel.

**Vitamin D** (N22 p. 20): D3 (cholecalciferol) is marked "Animal source"; D2 (ergocalciferol) "Plant source"; D3 from lichen or algae is listed, and "the species of lichen/algae shall need prior approval of Food Authority". This decides the veg mark.

**Note 1** (p. 23): suitable esters, derivatives and salts of vitamins and salts and chelates of minerals may be used; the FBO notifies the Food Authority in writing when it uses them (since 01.06.2026 through ePAAS, Order of 06.05.2026).

## 3. Schedule II, plants and botanicals

Structural rules (N22 5(2)(a) footnote, p. 6; Schedule II notes, p. 58; N16 7(3)(v), p. 13), all audit points:

1. The permitted range is given **in terms of raw herb or material**, for adults per day.
2. An extract may be used, subject to the **extractive ratio in relation to the daily usage value**, and **only from the part of the plant listed** in the Schedule.
3. Note 1: offering these ingredients alone or in combination, as such or minimally processed (cleaned, de-weeded, sorted, dried or powdered), is **not permitted**.
4. Note 2: where more than one Schedule II ingredient is used, the ranges may be reduced or altered on scientific rationale, which the FBO must provide.
5. Note 3: the ingredients must comply with the Contaminants Regulations.
6. Note 4: children 5 to 16 years, one half of the adult range. Note 5: children 2 to 5 years, one quarter.
7. **Plant part on the label.** FSSAI's Advisory of 15.10.2026 (library 08.1, p. 2, para 6) directs "mandatory labelling indicating plant part used" together with the caution statement set against the botanical. So a botanical declaration is defective unless the label shows the **botanical name, the part used, and the extract ratio or standardisation**. "Rose Hip Extract 100 mg" answers none of the three.
8. **Caution statements.** Where the Schedule entry carries a caution ("Not recommended during pregnancy", "Not recommended for children below 5 years" and the like), the label must carry it: N22 5(12)(a)(ii)(F), N16 6(3)(iii)(g) or 7(4)(iii)(h), and the Advisory of 15.10.2026. FSSAI treats a missing statement as a breach of s. 23, actionable under s. 52.

### Ashwagandha

N22 Sch. II entry 432 (p. 57), N16 Sch. IV entry 432 (p. 112): **root**, 3 to 6 g powder; extract 0.5 to 1 g. FSSAI's Advisory of 16.04.2026 (library 08.1, p. 1, paras 2 and 4) states that only the roots and their extracts are permitted and that **leaves in crude, extract or any other form are not**. So:

- Ashwagandha leaf or leaf extract, in any form: **NC**, citing the Advisory of 16.04.2026 with N22 5(2)(a) footnote and N16 7(3)(v).
- Ashwagandha declared without the part: NC under rule 7 above, and the leaf question goes on the Open Verification List (certificate of analysis and supplier specification naming the part).
- "Whole plant" Ashwagandha: NC, because whole plant includes the leaf.

### The collagen trap

- **Part A entry 37, undenatured type II collagen, capped at 40 mg per day.** Native collagen working by oral tolerance (UC-II and equivalents).

A label reading "Collagen Peptide (Type II) 40 mg" straddles the two: "peptide" points to Part B, 40 mg is the Part A signature. Call for the ingredient specification in writing; the answer changes the permitted daily dose of the whole product.

## 6. Animal origin and the veg or non-veg mark

**The definition decides.** L&D 2(1)(o) (LD pp. 2 to 3): non-vegetarian food contains whole or part of any animal, including birds, insects, fresh water or marine animals, or eggs or products of any animal origin, **but does not include milk, milk products, honey or bees wax or carnauba wax or shellac**.

These force the brown triangle, unless the client produces a certificate of fermentation, synthetic or plant origin: gelatin and gelatin capsules (Letter of 18.09.2019: a green dot on gelatin-shell products is misbranding); collagen of every type and its hydrolysates; chondroitin sulphate (bovine, porcine, shark); glucosamine from crustacean shell (Part B entry 56 lists crustacean shells as a source); fish oil, krill oil and marine omega-3; shark cartilage and squalene; Vitamin D3 of animal source (N22 Sch. I, p. 20); egg-derived lysozyme and egg albumin; carmine and cochineal colour; royal jelly and propolis (bee products other than honey and beeswax are not excluded by the definition); lanolin-derived vitamins.

These do **not** by themselves force the brown triangle, because the definition excludes them: lactose, whey, casein and other milk products, including bovine colostrum; honey; beeswax; carnauba wax; shellac glaze.

**Compound ingredients.** By the Order of 05.04.2022 (library 08.2), the non-veg mark is required if any constituent of a compound ingredient is of animal origin, irrespective of its percentage.

Hyaluronic acid and sodium hyaluronate can be either: bacterial fermentation (Streptococcus zooepidemicus) is vegetarian; rooster comb extraction is not. Ask.

**Allergens are separate.** Milk products (lactose, whey, casein, colostrum), fish (fish oil, fish collagen), crustacean (shell glucosamine), egg, soy (lecithin, soy protein) and wheat trigger the allergen declaration under L&D 5(14) (LD pp. 18 to 19) whether or not they affect the veg mark.
